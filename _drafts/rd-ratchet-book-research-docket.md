# R&D Ratchet book research docket

**Purpose:** Convert the public 17-article series into a book without allowing narrative confidence to outrun evidence. The website should continue to carry roughly 60–70 percent of the final substance. The book earns its additional length through archives, interviews, comparative cases, longitudinal data, legal and budget detail, and full-page visual reasoning—not by withholding the articles' central argument.

## Claim registry and burden of proof

Each book claim must remain in one of five states:

1. **Documented fact:** supported by a primary record or a carefully bounded authoritative dataset.
2. **Firsthand observation:** limited to what the author directly encountered; it does not establish confidential motives, organization-wide state, or causal effect.
3. **Inference:** a mechanism consistent with evidence and named alternatives, not a fact inherited from the source beside it.
4. **Comparative finding:** supported by a predeclared comparison with an explicit unit, counterfactual, and sensitivity analysis.
5. **Design hypothesis:** a proposed institution, threshold, or budget rule with failure criteria and a revision path.

An assertion may move upward only when new evidence satisfies the stronger state's burden. Book prose must not silently promote an inference into a documented fact because the historical narrative became smoother.

## Dataset program

### 1. Capability balance-sheet panel

Unit: a bounded technical capability within an institution, observed annually. Record personnel by critical role, apprenticeship depth, reusable tools, code/data buildability, unique facilities, problem-initiation share, protected internal time, transition owners, external users, correction record, and estimated reconstitution time. Keep every dimension visible; do not collapse it into one prestige score.

Comparison targets should include capabilities that strengthened, weakened, transferred, dispersed successfully, and became obsolete. The framework fails if its indicators do not predict later external technical tests, transition, or reconstitution cost better than conventional inputs and outputs.

### 2. Charter-to-work FFRDC panel

For each current center, preserve its official activity class and sponsor relationship. Record five- and ten-year changes in task mix, who originates questions, researcher-initiated work, challenge/dissent channels, technical staffing, internal capability renewal, reusable assets, ordinary-procurement alternatives, sponsor use of deliverables, and successor-cohort formation. Systems engineering is not coded as research decline merely because it is engineering; drift is defined relative to charter and demonstrated public need.

### 3. Research-program afterlife panel

Sample completed ARPA-style and conventional programs. At program end and at two-, five-, and ten-year intervals, record buildable artifacts, artifact custodians, surviving teams and cross-team edges, follow-on funding source, mission adoption, standards, companies, abandoned paths, negative-result availability, and reconstitution cost. Compare technical ambition and selection because programs with harder goals should not be penalized for lower raw survival.

### 4. University total-mission-cost sample

Separate negotiated F&A rate, effective recovery, unrecovered shared cost, direct scientific cost, proposal and compliance labor, facility depreciation/renewal, and sponsor-imposed transaction cost. Measure time as well as dollars. Compare alternative award designs on total mission cost and scientific outcome, not on the indirect-cost rate alone.

### 5. Epistemic dependency pilots

Build three bounded claim graphs: one formal-mathematics corpus, one computational/software corpus, and one experimental literature with costly measurement. Record exact claim versions, available witnesses, reproductions, independent replications, disputes, downstream dependencies, corrections, and notification latency. The aim is not a universal error percentage; it is to test whether dependency-aware verification prevents repeated work or consequential reliance.

- Contemporaneous organization charts, staffing by technical role, internal research-initiation records, facilities investment, apprenticeship programs, technical memos, and product/mission handoff records for selected Bell-family periods.
- Public acquisition, donation, merger, annual-report, sponsor, and integration records for PARC–SRI and the announced IBM–HRL transaction, while treating post-transaction capability as an open longitudinal question.
- Program solicitations, performer lists, final reports, repositories, transition agreements, and follow-on awards for selected DARPA programs.
- FFRDC sponsoring agreements, comprehensive reviews, annual reports, task portfolios, staffing categories, sponsor-use records, and congressional oversight material.
- University negotiated-rate agreements, audited financial statements, facilities-condition reports, research-administration workload studies, award terms, and actual recovery data where public or voluntarily supplied.
- Retraction, correction, data/code, formalization, and independent replication records for the scientific-audit pilots.

## Interview roles

Interview by role and decision exposure, not prestige alone. Seek paired or conflicting accounts and give every consequential institutional claim a documentary or second-source path where possible.

- Former and current research staff, technical staff, machinists, research software engineers, facility operators, and apprentices.
- Research directors, line managers, finance and sponsored-project staff, product/mission transition owners, procurement officials, and program managers.
- People who left during a closure or merger and people who remained; people whose work transitioned and people whose technically sound work did not.
- FFRDC sponsor officials and center staff from R&D-laboratory, study-and-analysis, and systems-engineering categories.
- University principal investigators, lab managers, graduate students, postdocs, research administrators, facilities leaders, and agency cost negotiators.
- Verification researchers, journal or conference editors, formalization experts, experimental replicators, maintainers of scientific software, and downstream users affected by corrections.

Interview prompts should elicit decisions, alternatives, contemporaneous evidence, prediction records, and what would change the interviewee's interpretation. They should not ask for speculation about individual motives.

## Contradiction controls

- **Corporate science:** The book may argue decline in broad corporate publishing and institutional dispersion, but not disappearance of private R&D. Current business R&D scale and concentration are mandatory counterevidence.
- **Universities:** The book may argue incomplete incentive coverage, but not that universities cannot think long or that all overhead is waste. Real shared scientific costs and university counterexamples remain visible.
- **FFRDCs:** The book may argue role conversion in specific centers only against charter and longitudinal work evidence. Engineering, analysis, and sponsor memory can be the intended public capability.
- **Startups:** The book may argue that venture incentives do not replenish a broad commons, but not that commercialization or acquisition is inherently destructive.
- **Mergers and closures:** Reemployment, transaction completion, and name survival are neither proof of preservation nor proof of loss. The counterfactual capability graph is the unit of judgment.
- **AI verification:** A retraction or failed replication is not a field error rate. Model agreement is not independent evidence. Formal checking does not by itself establish semantic alignment or empirical truth.
- **Expert governance:** Practiced research judgment must enter decision rights, but publication count, fame, or technical authority cannot become unilateral rule over budgets, ethics, law, safety, or public priorities.

## Book go/no-go tests

The book should not claim a settled national capability decline unless at least one prospective or credible retrospective capability panel supports it beyond spending and output measures. If that evidence cannot be assembled, the book should remain explicit that it presents a system diagnosis and measurement program rather than a proven national time series.

The successor chapter should not present one institutional design as inevitable. It must compare the proposal with successful universities, corporate laboratories, FFRDCs, national laboratories, ARPA programs, consortia, open-source communities, and frontier companies. A pilot that cannot beat strong current arrangements on capability formation, correction, transition, and public value should be redesigned or ended.
