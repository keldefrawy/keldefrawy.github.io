# R&D Ratchet cross-series review of v0.1

Review date: 2026-07-24  
Scope: All seventeen article files, with revision authority limited to the thirteen new drafts (Articles 2–6 and 10–17). Articles 1, 7, 8, and 9 were treated as protected reference texts: no prose was removed or rewritten.  
Baseline: Exact v0.1 copies of the thirteen new drafts are preserved under `_drafts/rd-ratchet-draft-archive/v0-1/`.

## Review result

No irreconcilable thesis contradiction was found. The series consistently distinguishes R&D spending from retained capability, institutional names from working capability graphs, engineering from loss of research initiative, and model output from verification.

The review found three material corrections, six clarification needs, and several deliberate overlaps that should remain because the articles test the same mechanism from different institutional positions. The material corrections are:

1. Article 17’s central claim says AI will “only accelerate” fragmented-system failures. That is too absolute and conflicts with Article 16’s claim that AI can also scale verification. It must say that AI accelerates both productive work and the failure modes selected by existing incentives.
2. Article 13’s v0.1 evidence chart compares counts of legal-status categories. Although qualified, its bar lengths are not an illuminating empirical relationship. Replace it with HRL’s documented pre-transaction organizational baseline: four laboratories and five centers.
3. The 2023 PARC–SRI evidence boundary initially rested on an incorrect compressed chronology. The author subsequently clarified that Confidencial.io spun out in 2021, received seed financing in 2022, and overlapped with part-time SRI work through early 2023 while he finished DARPA programs; he retained an SRI office and badge through 2026. The event should remain Firsthand for bounded campus observation, while the legal transaction and any confidential integration decisions remain Documentary or outside the author’s direct knowledge.

## Falsifiers and limiting evidence

The series should weaken or reject its claims if longitudinal evidence shows that newer distributed arrangements consistently preserve or improve:

- team and apprenticeship continuity without durable institutional employment;
- independent correction despite common funding and data pipelines;
- reproducible artifacts and tacit-knowledge transfer across frequent organizational handoffs;
- transition rates without a named, funded transition owner;
- broad foundational research inside focused startups or sponsor-responsive engineering organizations without separate commons funding;
- capability formation despite stable or declining protected time, shared infrastructure, and problem-initiation authority.

The successor proposal should be rejected or redesigned if its pilot produces administrative load, incumbent capture, technical monoculture, weak transition, or protected research without hard external results at rates no better than existing institutions.

## Publication blockers that remain after v0.2

These drafts can be rendered as first drafts, but publication at v1.0 should still require:

1. interview and documentary corroboration for the firsthand Lucent, HRL, and SRI mechanisms;
2. reconciliation of employment-year labels on the separate public project record without guessing about project continuation after departure;
3. confirmation of the IBM/HRL transaction status immediately before publication;
4. current interviews or institutional data for the post-2023 SRI/PARC integration;
5. a legal and confidentiality review of firsthand passages and any future project-specific detail;
6. independent source audit of the author-supplied generative-model manuscript before it supports any stronger claim.
