## Acceptance tests

Every consequential revision should satisfy these tests:

1. **Evidence class:** The text distinguishes firsthand observation, institutional or government documentation, interview evidence, inference, and proposal.
2. **Claim boundary:** A source supports the precise sentence attached to it; adjacent claims do not inherit authority by proximity.
3. **Causal discipline:** Correlation, historical sequence, accounting identity, mechanism, and causal finding are not treated as synonyms.
4. **Adversarial strength:** The second-level objection must engage the response, and the surviving conclusion must be narrower than the opening claim when evidence requires it.
5. **Counterexample:** Major claims identify at least one serious case that could weaken or falsify them.
6. **Measurement:** Proposed latent concepts—capability, horizon, adjacency, memory, independence, transition—receive observable indicators and explicit failure conditions.
7. **No nostalgia shortcut:** Historical laboratories are judged by capabilities and failures, not by name, age, prestige, or awards alone.
8. **No individual targeting:** Criticism addresses incentives, governance, institutional behavior, or documented decisions without imputing character or private motive.
9. **AI specificity:** AI is separated into hypothesis generation, synthesis, formal checking, software execution, experimental control, provenance, and organizational memory; “AI” is not used as one undifferentiated capability.
10. **Policy operability:** Recommendations name the decision right, payer, time horizon, measurement method, failure mode, appeal path, and sunset or renewal rule.
11. **Web completeness:** The live article contains the core argument and evidence needed to evaluate it independently; book-only expansion is additive rather than corrective.
12. **Reading architecture:** Long additions use diagrams, tables, short propositions, and navigable sections so rigor does not become an undifferentiated wall of text.

## Book-level intellectual spine

The series should converge on a set of original but testable propositions:

- **Capability is a stock, expenditure is a flow.** Annual R&D accounts can rise while reusable institutional state depreciates.
- **The effective horizon is a minimum-cut property.** An effort lasts only as long as its shortest-lived non-transferable critical complement.
- **Adjacency is capital.** The option to recombine theory, experiment, engineering, manufacturing, operations, and use depends on living edges between roles, not a directory of individually excellent nodes.
- **Portfolio diversity must include failure-mode diversity.** Many projects do not create resilience when they share the same data, metrics, career filters, and transition bottlenecks.
- **Handoffs compound loss.** End-to-end survival depends on state-preserving transfers, so a sequence of individually acceptable handoffs can produce poor system reliability.
- **AI creates an abundance inversion.** Plausible hypotheses, prose, code, and scientific form become cheaper; independent measurement, checkable witnesses, scarce facilities, accountable judgment, and institutional custody become more valuable.
- **Verification is infrastructure.** The scientific record needs dependency-aware maintenance and correction, not merely more publications or one-time replication campaigns.
- **A project portfolio is not an institution.** Durable R&D also needs custodians for shared tools, negative results, apprenticeship, security, facilities, and paths to transition.
- **Long-horizon funding needs reversible authority.** Technical leaders require real discretion, but dissent, audit, portfolio plurality, and staged renewal must constrain expert capture.
- **Public value needs a residual claimant.** When spillovers escape every individual sponsor, an institution must be chartered and funded to own continuity on behalf of the public.

- Expand reproducibility evidence without converting retractions or convenience samples into population error rates.
- Distinguish formal proof checking, computational reproduction, empirical replication, robustness, and semantic alignment.
- Ground the AI-native successor in current statutory and institutional building blocks while stating what none of them supplies alone.

## Baseline audit

The automated baseline audit reports 17 articles, approximately 31,300 body words, 76 ledger sources, 76 unique article-declared sources, four-stage argument maps in every article, and two two-level objection ladders in every article. Source identifiers resolve internally with no undeclared or missing ledger entries.

The structural completeness is ahead of the intellectual depth. The priority defects are:

1. Twelve case and synthesis articles remain between roughly 1,150 and 1,750 body words; several state a good mechanism but do not yet test it against enough alternative explanations or cases.
2. Explicit falsification language is absent from most articles even when claim status is correctly marked provisional.
3. Several numeric paragraphs need closer documentary markers or source links; the audit flags these for sentence-level review rather than assuming they are unsupported.
4. The four strongest original concepts—capability stock, capability cut, common-mode portfolio error, and epistemic debt—are not yet defined in one shared measurement vocabulary.
5. FFRDC criticism is responsibly bounded but too compressed; the series needs a stronger typology separating research, systems engineering, test and evaluation, acquisition support, and sponsor-memory functions.
6. The historical cases rely heavily on transaction announcements and institutional accounts. Those records establish what was announced, not what capability survived; post-event outcome evidence remains a declared gap.
7. Current corporate counterexamples—especially frontier AI, semiconductor, and pharmaceutical laboratories—need to be treated as tests of the thesis rather than omitted as inconvenient cases.
8. Article 17 has a credible institutional outline but needs decision-rights diagrams, a capability-accounting instrument, budget ranges, anti-capture rules, and explicit comparative baselines.
9. The hero illustrations are distinct, but visual reasoning inside the articles still needs a consistent book-level grammar and provenance convention.
