Before reading · A note to colleagues

This is a critique of systems, not the people inside them.

Throughout this journey I worked with researchers, engineers, technicians, program managers, administrative and support staff, and institutional leaders who cared deeply about the work—and who often spent considerable effort protecting it. I learned from them and owe them a real intellectual debt.

This series is not an indictment of those people, of everyone who worked at a named laboratory, or of all work performed under a surviving institutional name. Its subject is the harder systemic problem: capable, conscientious people can be constrained by budgets, reorganizations, procurement rules, product horizons, and governance structures that no single person controls. Those pressures can weaken collective research capability even while individuals continue to do excellent work.

Gratitude is not immunity from scrutiny, and scrutiny does not erase gratitude. The criticism here addresses evidence about institutional capabilities, incentives, decisions, and destructive behaviors—not private motives, character, or collective blame.
